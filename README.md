# CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle

## SNHU Travel Project

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective for the SNHU Travel project in CS 250 at Southern New Hampshire University. The course used a simulated Scrum team to explore how user needs, changing requirements, development, and testing connect throughout the software development lifecycle.

## Project Artifact

[SNHU Travel Sprint Review and Retrospective](SNHU%20Travel%20Retrospective.pdf)

The retrospective discusses Scrum roles, completing user stories, adapting to the change from a destination list to a slideshow, communication, and improvements I would apply in a future Sprint.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by identifying what users want to accomplish, asking questions about unclear requirements, and confirming priorities with the Product Owner. User stories help me connect a requested feature to the person who needs it and the benefit it should provide. In the SNHU Travel simulation, customer requests for ranked destinations, personalized recommendations, and price limits provided a starting point for defining features and acceptance criteria. I then connect those criteria to development tasks and observable tests, such as checking that a price filter excludes packages outside the selected range. When the interface changed to a slideshow, revising the navigation tests helped preserve the underlying user need to browse destinations.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by clarifying requirements, breaking work into manageable tasks, and checking each increment against its expected behavior. This course showed me the value of revisiting assumptions when requirements change and updating tests alongside the implementation. In future work, I hope to use an ordered Product Backlog, Sprint Planning, regular backlog refinement, and short development cycles to maintain clear priorities. On a team, I would use the Daily Scrum to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt our plan. Sprint Reviews would help us gather feedback about the product, while Retrospectives would help us improve how we work. I would also record actual test outcomes and clarify edge cases, such as navigation at the first and last slides, before treating a feature as complete.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, following through on commitments, and helping the team achieve a shared goal. It also means asking specific questions early, listening to different perspectives, and making blockers or scope changes visible before they create unnecessary rework. My simulated developer and tester communications emphasized confirming priorities, acceptance criteria, and expected results with the appropriate people. In a real team, I would document those decisions beside the related backlog items, share useful feedback respectfully, and take responsibility for the quality of our work. A strong teammate remains open to change and helps turn feedback into practical improvements.
